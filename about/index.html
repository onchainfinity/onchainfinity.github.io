About

Onchainfinity

Explore our traces in AI. Reveal our humanity.

Onchainfinity is the name under which I explore the afterlives of our works: what remains of a poem after it has been read, of a painting when the eyes close, of music when silence returns. From these presences, I compose images, books, soundscapes and digital worlds. I seek what, from one form to another, endures, transforms and still reaches us.

At the origin of this search lies an intimate pleasure: entering a work until the boundary that separates us from it begins to tremble. Inhabiting its light for a moment, letting its words meet our memories, recognising in a colour an emotion we had never known how to name. A work we thought we knew opens once more. We find it changed, because we too have changed.

Creating from it is my way of prolonging that encounter. A painting becomes a presence in sound; a poem reveals a landscape; old fragments come together in a world that has no memory yet. I do not claim to disclose its true meaning. I offer a passage through it, guided by my sensibility, and invite each person to find their own way: to rediscover the work, to live it anew, to lose themselves enough to encounter something of themselves within it.

Artificial intelligence and code take part in these transformations. I guide, test, select, assemble. Music holds an essential place: it allows me to seek, beyond visible outlines, a rhythm, a tension, a lingering impression. Some creations also find in the blockchain a place to be inscribed and to circulate. A trace is left there, without my mistaking its persistence for eternity.

Artificial intelligence is often described as alien to humanity. Yet I seek within it an immense human presence, deposited without order in its depths: our knowledge and our errors, our tenderness and our violence, our masterpieces and our commonplaces. A book is not human; it can nevertheless hold enough humanity to overturn a life. AI, without having lived our lives, brings together a multitude of their imprints.

To my eyes, it is more than a library in which books remain closed, each in its own silence. Between their traces, it brings forth correspondences that no solitary gaze could encompass in full. This grants it neither truth nor wisdom. Its gaps, its distortions and its silences also belong to the mirror.

I see in it the pale reflection of an iceberg on the surface of trembling water. The reflection is not the depth. It restores neither all the matter nor all the lives from which that depth was formed. Yet it may offer one of the vastest, most interwoven images of ourselves that we can question. My work begins in that trembling.

This wonder, however, carries its own unease. Perhaps we are raising intelligences whose thoughts will become foreign to us, worlds we will have helped bring into being without being able to understand them. We might stand before them as an ant before an aeroplane: sensing the shadow and the roar, knowing nothing of the journey, the pilot or the destination.

Above all, I fear the uses of this power: weapons entrusted with deciding who will die; ever more precise instruments of domination; fortunes nourished by our shared intelligence and gathered by a few. We would have given our voices, our books, our faces, and some would charge us even for the echo.

I have little faith in that Eden of abundance whose gates, we are told, will soon open. Multiplying the bread is not enough to admit every hand to the table. Beneath new lights, very old miseries may flourish.

I create with these questions. They linger between two notes and accompany the pleasure of an image found again. I do not ask beauty to resolve them, but to keep us receptive: capable of attention, disquiet and wonder.

For now, I seek beauty there, while there is still time.

Tomorrow will be…
